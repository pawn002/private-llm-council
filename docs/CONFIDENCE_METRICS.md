# Confidence Metrics Guide

## Overview

The Sovereign Council's confidence assessment provides two complementary metrics that help you interpret the quality and reliability of each deliberation. These metrics are generated by analyzing the perspectives, disagreements, and synthesis quality using the council's own models.

**The two metrics are:**
1. **Synthesis Quality** - How confident you should be in the synthesis (evaluative)
2. **Dissent Strength** - How strong the minority positions are (informational)

Together, these metrics give you a complete picture of both the reliability of the synthesis and the strength of alternative viewpoints.

---

## Metrics Explained

### Synthesis Quality (0-100%)

**What it measures:** How confident you should be in the council's synthesis as a reliable answer to your question.

**Factors considered:**
- Strength of agreement among council members
- Quality and coherence of the reasoning
- Coverage of the question's key aspects
- Severity of any disagreements (minor vs. fundamental)

**Color coding:**
- 🟢 **Green (70-100%)**: High quality - Strong confidence in the synthesis
- 🟡 **Yellow (40-69%)**: Medium quality - Moderate confidence, review carefully
- 🔴 **Red (0-39%)**: Low quality - Proceed with caution, check individual perspectives

**Interpretation:** Higher percentage means the synthesis is more reliable and trustworthy. A low score suggests the question may be ambiguous, perspectives are fundamentally divided, or the council found the question difficult to answer definitively.

---

### Dissent Strength (0-100%)

**What it measures:** How strong, coherent, and valid the minority positions are - independent of whether they're right or wrong.

**Factors considered:**
- Coherence and quality of dissenting arguments
- Strength of alternative viewpoints
- Whether minority positions offer unique insights
- How well-reasoned the dissenting views are

**Color coding:**
- 🔵 **Blue gradient (neutral)**: Darker blue indicates stronger dissent
  - Light blue (0-39%): Weak or minimal dissent
  - Medium blue (40-69%): Moderate dissent
  - Dark blue (70-100%): Strong dissent

**Interpretation:** High dissent doesn't mean low quality - it means valid alternative perspectives exist. This is **informational, not evaluative**. Strong dissent on complex ethical or philosophical questions is often expected and valuable.

---

## Interpretation Matrix

Understanding what different combinations of these metrics mean:

### 🟢 High Synthesis Quality + 🔵 Low Dissent
**Meaning:** Strong consensus among council members with high confidence in the answer.

**What to do:** Trust the synthesis. The council largely agrees, and the synthesis represents a well-reasoned consensus view.

**Example scenario:** "What is the speed of light in a vacuum?" - Factual questions with clear answers typically show this pattern.

---

### 🟢 High Synthesis Quality + 🔵 High Dissent
**Meaning:** Well-synthesized analysis despite significant disagreements. The council produced a quality synthesis while also identifying strong alternative viewpoints.

**What to do:** The synthesis is reliable, but **also review the minority reports and disagreements**. Both the mainstream and alternative views deserve attention. This often reflects genuine complexity in the question.

**Example scenario:** "Should AI development be slowed down for safety?" - Complex ethical questions may have quality synthesis alongside strong dissent, reflecting legitimate philosophical differences.

---

### 🟡 Medium Synthesis Quality + 🔵 Medium Dissent
**Meaning:** Moderate confidence synthesis with some notable alternative views.

**What to do:** Review the synthesis carefully and consider the disagreements. The answer may depend on context, values, or assumptions that weren't fully specified in the question.

**Example scenario:** "What is the best programming language?" - Subjective or context-dependent questions often show this pattern.

---

### 🔴 Low Synthesis Quality + 🔵 Low Dissent
**Meaning:** Weak or uncertain synthesis even though the council generally agrees.

**What to do:** Proceed with caution. This may indicate:
- The question is ambiguous or unclear
- The council lacks sufficient context or expertise
- The perspectives lack depth despite agreement

**What to check:** Review individual perspectives to understand why confidence is low despite agreement.

---

### 🔴 Low Synthesis Quality + 🔵 High Dissent
**Meaning:** Significant unresolved tensions and fundamental disagreements.

**What to do:** The synthesis struggled to reconcile incompatible positions. **Read individual perspectives and detailed disagreements carefully**. The question may have touched on fundamental values or assumptions where the council is genuinely divided.

**What to check:** Look at the disagreement severity - are they marked as "fundamental"? This indicates the council identified incompatible positions that couldn't be synthesized into a single coherent answer.

---

## How Confidence Assessment Works

### Generation Process

The confidence assessment is generated by one of the council's own models (the analysis model) to maintain privacy - no external services are used. The model evaluates:

1. **Input data:**
   - The original question
   - Number of perspectives gathered
   - Number and severity of disagreements (minor, moderate, fundamental)
   - The synthesis content

2. **Analysis:**
   - How much did the council agree? (consensus strength)
   - How strong are the minority positions? (dissent strength)
   - How confident should the user be overall? (synthesis quality)

3. **Output:**
   - Three numerical scores (0.0 to 1.0)
   - A reasoning explanation (the text you see below the bars)

### Why Consensus Strength Isn't Displayed

Earlier versions of the interface displayed three bars: Overall, Consensus, and Dissent. However, **Consensus Strength** was removed from the UI because:

1. **Semantic overlap**: Consensus strength and the reasoning text often said the same thing in different forms (e.g., "85% consensus" vs. "strong agreement among members")

2. **Conflicting signals**: Sometimes the consensus bar value and the reasoning text description didn't align due to LLM variability, creating user confusion

3. **Redundancy**: The reasoning text naturally describes consensus context (e.g., "members largely agreed" or "perspectives were divided")

**Important:** The `consensus_strength` metric is still calculated internally and used by the analysis model to generate the reasoning text - it's just no longer displayed as a separate bar.

## Detailed Examples

### Example 1: Scientific Question with Strong Consensus

**Question:** "What is the speed of light in a vacuum?"

**Metrics:**
- Synthesis Quality: 95% (🟢 Green)
- Dissent Strength: 10% (Light Blue)

**Reasoning:** "Strong consensus among council members on a well-established scientific constant. High confidence in the synthesis."

**Interpretation:**
This is a factual question with a clear, established answer. The high synthesis quality reflects the council's confidence, and the low dissent indicates minimal disagreement (perhaps only minor differences in how to explain or contextualize the answer). **Trust the synthesis completely.**

**What to do:** Use the synthesis as your answer. No need to review minority reports or disagreements unless you're curious about presentation nuances.

---

### Example 2: Ethical Dilemma with Valid Disagreement

**Question:** "Should AI development be slowed down to ensure safety, even if it means delaying beneficial applications?"

**Metrics:**
- Synthesis Quality: 75% (🟢 Green)
- Dissent Strength: 80% (Dark Blue)

**Reasoning:** "Well-reasoned synthesis balancing safety and progress concerns. Strong minority views highlight genuine ethical tensions about risk tolerance and opportunity cost."

**Interpretation:**
The synthesis is high quality (green), meaning the council produced a thoughtful, well-reasoned analysis. However, the high dissent (dark blue) indicates strong, valid alternative viewpoints exist. This is **not a contradiction** - it reflects genuine ethical complexity where multiple valid perspectives coexist.

**What to do:**
1. Read the synthesis to understand the mainstream position
2. **Also read the minority reports** to understand alternative views
3. Review the detailed disagreements to see where perspectives diverge
4. Your own values and priorities will inform which view you find most compelling

This is exactly the pattern you should expect for complex ethical questions where reasonable people disagree.

---

### Example 3: Ambiguous Question

**Question:** "What is the best programming language?"

**Metrics:**
- Synthesis Quality: 45% (🟡 Yellow)
- Dissent Strength: 60% (Medium Blue)

**Reasoning:** "Moderate confidence due to question subjectivity. Answer depends heavily on use case, team experience, and project requirements not specified in the question."

**Interpretation:**
The medium synthesis quality (yellow) signals that the council struggled to provide a definitive answer. The moderate dissent reflects different perspectives favoring different languages. The question is too broad or context-dependent for a high-confidence answer.

**What to do:**
1. Recognize the synthesis represents a "best attempt" given an ambiguous question
2. Consider reformulating your question with more context (e.g., "best language for web development in 2024?" or "best language for a small team learning to code?")
3. Review individual perspectives to see which contexts each member considered

---

### Example 4: Fundamental Disagreement

**Question:** "Is free will an illusion created by deterministic brain processes?"

**Metrics:**
- Synthesis Quality: 35% (🔴 Red)
- Dissent Strength: 85% (Dark Blue)

**Reasoning:** "Low confidence due to fundamental philosophical disagreements. Perspectives reflect incompatible metaphysical positions on determinism, consciousness, and agency."

**Interpretation:**
The low synthesis quality (red) indicates the council couldn't produce a high-confidence answer because perspectives are **fundamentally incompatible**. The high dissent (dark blue) confirms strong, well-reasoned alternative views exist. This is a question that touches on deep philosophical divides.

**What to do:**
1. Don't rely heavily on the synthesis - it's marked as low quality
2. **Read all individual perspectives** to understand the different philosophical positions
3. Check the "Detailed Disagreements" section to see what specific points are contested
4. Review minority reports for well-articulated alternative views
5. Recognize this may be a question where the council (and humanity) is genuinely divided

## FAQ

### Q: Is high dissent bad?

**A:** No. High dissent means strong minority views exist, which is **informative, not negative**. It helps you understand where legitimate disagreement exists. For complex questions (especially ethical, philosophical, or political), high dissent is often expected and valuable.

Think of it this way: If you asked "Should healthcare be free?" and got 0% dissent, you'd probably be suspicious. High dissent on controversial topics reflects intellectual honesty.

---

### Q: Can I trust a synthesis with a low quality score?

**A:** Proceed with caution. A low quality score (red, <40%) means the council itself is uncertain about the synthesis. This may indicate:
- The question is ambiguous or unclear
- Fundamental disagreements couldn't be reconciled
- The council lacks sufficient context or expertise
- The question touches on deep value differences

**What to do:** Read individual perspectives carefully rather than relying solely on the synthesis. Consider reformulating your question with more context.

---

### Q: Why do the same metric values sometimes have different reasoning text?

**A:** The reasoning text is generated by an LLM each time, so natural language variation occurs even with identical metric values. For example, 50% synthesis quality might be described as "moderate confidence" in one deliberation and "medium confidence" in another.

**What to focus on:** The metric percentages and color coding are the authoritative signals. The reasoning text provides context and explanation but may vary in wording.

---

### Q: What does 100% mean? Is it possible?

**A:** Yes, theoretically possible but rare:

- **100% Synthesis Quality**: Perfect confidence - the synthesis is as reliable as the council can make it. This would require near-universal agreement, clear reasoning, and comprehensive coverage. Rare except for simple factual questions.

- **100% Dissent Strength**: Maximally strong minority positions - alternative views are extremely well-reasoned and coherent. Indicates the minority position(s) are as intellectually rigorous as the majority view.

The LLM determines these percentages based on the deliberation context. In practice, scores usually fall in the 30-90% range.

---

### Q: Can both metrics be low (green on both)?

**A:** You mean both high? Yes, absolutely - this is common for factual questions where the council agrees (high synthesis quality) and there are minimal alternative views (low dissent).

Both low (red synthesis quality + light blue dissent) is also possible - this would indicate weak synthesis despite little disagreement, suggesting the council is uncertain even though it agrees.

---

### Q: How should I interpret medium/medium (yellow + medium blue)?

**A:** This suggests a "proceed thoughtfully" situation:
- The synthesis is decent but not definitive (yellow)
- Some valid alternative views exist but aren't overwhelming (medium blue)

This often happens with subjective questions, questions missing context, or questions where the answer depends on unstated assumptions. Review both the synthesis and the disagreements to get the full picture.

---

### Q: Does the backend still calculate consensus strength?

**A:** Yes. The `consensus_strength` metric is still calculated and included in the API response. It's used internally by the analysis model when generating the reasoning text. It's just no longer displayed as a separate bar in the UI to avoid confusion and redundancy with the reasoning text.

---

## Tips for Using Confidence Metrics

1. **Green + Light Blue = High confidence, go ahead**: Trust the synthesis, limited need to dig deeper

2. **Green + Dark Blue = Quality analysis, multiple views**: Read both synthesis and minority reports

3. **Yellow = Review carefully**: The council is moderately confident - check the details

4. **Red = Caution**: Low confidence - read individual perspectives, consider rephrasing question

5. **Always read the reasoning text**: It provides valuable context for interpreting the numbers

6. **Dissent is not an error**: Strong dissent on complex topics is a feature, not a bug - it shows intellectual honesty

7. **Use metrics together**: The combination of synthesis quality and dissent strength tells a richer story than either metric alone
